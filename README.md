# Hazna

A sorting puzzle for Android, set in a padishah's treasury. Each kind of gem goes into its own sack, filled to the brim, drawstring pulled tight — and the sacks are cut to the count of the stones, so nothing fits by accident.

Every rule, every pixel of layout, every byte of logic was generated by AI (**Claude Opus 5.5**) through plain conversation. Not a single line was written by a human hand. Plain Java, no libraries, no build system.

**Package:** `io.github.shumtugle.hazna` · **Version:** 2.0.0 · **Site:** [shumtugle.github.io/hazna](https://shumtugle.github.io/hazna/)

![The padishah on his throne and the vizier at the foot of the steps](cover-square.jpg)

## The rules

- **Sacks are cut to the count** — as many places as there are stones of that kind. No slack, no crowding.
- **Like to like, then seal.** A full sack closes itself.
- **Two sets of rules.** Under **strict** rules (default) a seal is final: a sack closed on the wrong stones stays closed, and one careless move can cost the whole audit. Under **forgiving** rules a tap tears the seal off and puts the sack back to work. Switch in settings.
- **The chest cannot be sealed.** It takes in what has no place yet, and by the last knot it must stand empty.
- **Nothing finishes itself.** The final stone goes in by your hand — there is no auto-complete.
- Every **tenth** audit ends with a remark from the padishah, a hundred of them in rotation. Every **thirtieth**, a painting for five seconds.

## How it thinks

**The climb.** Difficulty rises one step every three audits — a new kind of stone, then wider sacks — until it meets the ceiling of what the screen can hold. Every twentieth audit is one step easier, every thirtieth two. The rebate is taken *after* the ceiling clamp, not before it: otherwise the breathers would vanish the moment the raw curve outgrew the ceiling.

**Deals are unwound, not guessed.** A level is produced by taking a sealed treasury apart with reversible moves. Every step is invertible by construction, so a way back always exists — an unwinnable board cannot occur, and the dealer needs no solver to vet itself. Under strict rules the way back must also avoid seals, so the dealer refuses any reverse move that would leave a sack full and uniform.

**Hint and Solve** use a priority search with an estimate of the work remaining, on a background thread. Under forgiving rules, when a handful can be poured whole onto its own kind, only such moves are considered. Under strict rules that shortcut can cut off the only path, so the search prunes traps instead: a move that seals a sack on less than the whole kind can never lead to a win.

**Dead ends are bisected.** A position with no way out stays that way after any move, so the history splits cleanly in two. The last salvageable point is found by bisection; Solve winds the stones back to that fork and plays on from there.

**Sound is synthesised** in code when the game starts. No audio files ship with the app.

**Progress** — audit number, chests sealed, moves in all — stays on the device. The board itself is never saved. Updating from 1.x keeps it.

## Languages

English is built in. Every other language is a **module**: one plain text file, opened with the game or loaded in settings. Thirteen are ready in [`modules/`](modules/): Russian complete, twelve more with the interface and the tale. A missing word stays English, so a half-filled module already works. The format and the rules for translators are in [`modules/README.md`](modules/README.md).

## Permissions

None. No internet, no location, no storage, no accounts, no ads, no analytics. The game runs fully offline. A language file is read once, when you hand it over.

build.sh              checks, compile, dex, package, align, sign

## Building

```sh
SDK=path/to/android-34.jar KEYSTORE=path/to/release.keystore KS_PASS_FILE=path/to/password.txt sh build.sh
```

Needs a JDK and the platform's command-line packager, dexer, aligner and signer on `PATH`. The script runs the checks first and stops if one fails; the signed APK lands in `out/`.

## Playing without Android

[`play.html`](https://shumtugle.github.io/hazna/play.html) is version 1.2.1, the last one written for the browser: one self-contained file, fourteen languages built in. It is kept as it was and no longer changes.
