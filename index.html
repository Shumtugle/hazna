The story

He wanted the limit, not the sum

Padishah Murr ad-Din put no faith in the calligraphy of his scribes: paper endures any lie, provided the lie is tidy. He wanted something a paw could rest on.

The seals were broken. Eleven years of disorder spilled out of the dark — ruby pressed under the dull weight of onyx, pearl rubbed raw among spinel, and on the sapphires a hollow left by whoever had slept there while the realm was called rich. Drawing the border of all that splendour falls to you.

The rules

The matter sets them, not the designer

  • Tap a sack to lift it, tap another to pour. The top handful of one kind moves, as much as fits.
  • Sacks are cut to the count. As many places as there are stones of that kind — no slack, no crowding.
  • Like to like, then seal. A full sack pulls its own drawstring and takes a wax stamp.
  • Two sets of rules. Strict: the seal is final, and one careless move can cost the audit. Forgiving: a tap tears the seal off again. Strict is the default.
  • The chest cannot be sealed. It is only a place to set things down while counting, and by the last knot it must stand empty.
  • Nothing finishes itself. The last stone goes in by your hand — no auto-complete, ever.
  • Stuck? Hint lifts the two sacks of a good move. Solve plays the audit out — and from a dead end, winds back to where it still added up.

Languages

English inside, every other language a file

How to

Changing the language

  1. Download the file on the phone: tap a language above. It lands in your downloads.
  2. Open it with Hazna. Tap the finished download, or find the file in any file manager, and choose Hazna. The game shows the language's name and how many words it fills — tap Add.
  3. Or from inside the game. Tap the amulet — the eye in the top corner — then Load from file under Language, and pick the file.
  4. Switch any time. The amulet opens settings from every screen; tap a language to use it. Automatic follows the phone's own language when that module is installed, and English otherwise.
  5. Remove a language with a long press on its button.

Updating from 1.x? Your audit number, seals and moves come along. If you played in Russian, it switches back on by itself the moment the Russian module is added.

Translators

Make a language of your own

Download the template and open it in any text editor. Each entry is the English source in a comment, then a key, a tab and your words — fill the space after the tab. Keep the first line as it is, write your language's name after module and its short code after code. Leave a line empty and the game simply says it in English.

How it thinks

No unwinnable board can exist

Deals are unwound, not guessed

A level is made by taking a sealed treasury apart with reversible moves. Every step is invertible by construction, so a way back always exists. The dealer never needs a solver to vet its own work.

Three audits to a step

A new kind of stone every third audit, then wider sacks, until the screen runs out of room. Every twentieth audit comes one step easier, every thirtieth two — the rebate is taken after the ceiling, so the breathers survive on the plateau.

Beatable at the start, either way

Under strict rules the way back must avoid seals too, so the dealer refuses any reverse move that would leave a sack full and uniform. Only your own hand can ruin a deal.

Solve bisects your history

A position with no way out stays that way after any move. So the history splits cleanly in two, and the last salvageable point is found by bisection — a handful of probes instead of one per move.

Thinking off the main thread

Hint and Solve search on a background thread with a deadline. The board keeps breathing, and Stop really stops.

Sound out of nothing

The stone's knock and the seal's thud are synthesised in code the first time the game starts. Not one audio file ships with the app.

Between audits

The padishah watches, and now and then speaks

Every tenth audit closes with a remark from the throne — a hundred of them, enough for a thousand chests. “Your predecessor kept records. The records burned. So did he.”

Every thirtieth, a painting fills the screen for five seconds: the vizier at his maps, the scales, the seal and the pigeon, the treasury clockwork.

Permissions

None. Not one.

No internet, no location, no storage, no accounts, no ads, no analytics. A language file is read once, when you hand it over. Progress — the audit number, chests sealed, moves in all — never leaves the device, and the board itself is not even saved.

How it's made

Plain Java, one script

Since 2.0 the game is native: twelve Java classes, the board painted stroke by stroke, no libraries, no build system — only the platform's own command-line tools. The rules engine is pure Java and is checked on a desktop before every build: nine hundred deals solved, thirty dead ends bisected, every language module held against the template.
